Supermarket Confusion - Getting the most from your yogurt

Posted by cdombrow on 2013-08-6 9:31

How many times do you stand in front of a wall of food products at the supermarket trying to make a choice?  It certainly can be confusing. 

I work on the Heart and Stroke Foundation’s Health Check™ Program that is designed to make the healthy choice, the easy choice.  When you see the Health Check symbol on food products you know that these products have been evaluated by a Heart and Stroke Foundation dietitian and are part of a healthy diet.  The problem is that there are lots of products in the supermarket that haven’t been evaluated as yet.  Today, I am offering to help you as I attempt to take the confusion out of the ‘yogurt wall’.

The basics on yogurt:

Yogurt provides us with many of the same nutrients as milk, including calcium and protein, and when fortified milk is used to make the yogurt also Vitamin D.  Fat and sugar are the real variables.  You can buy yogurt with zero per cent fat or as much as 11% fat.  The sugar content can be all over the map.  Plain yogurt contains about 8 grams of sugar per 175 g, which is naturally occurring from the milk. Once you start adding flavourings and sweetener, the amount of sugar grows substantially, from 11g to 32g, with the average amount being at 22g of sugar per 175 g. Yogurt containers really vary in size.  The first thing you need to do when looking at the Nutrition Facts table is to check the serving size.  

Get ‘em to the Greek:

Greek Yogurt is everywhere.  The extra protein boost you get from Greek Yogurt makes it my favourite way to start the day. You will get about double the amount of protein in Greek Yogurt as compared to regular yogurt.   I have Plain Greek Yogurt, topped with blueberries and a whole grain cereal for breakfast most days.  Again you will find a wide variety of types of Greek Yogurt in yogurt wall.  This category seems to be growing daily. 

Best advice:

Choose plain, lower fat regular or Greek yogurt and add your own fruit. Experiment and find the brand of yogurt that you like the taste of best.

If you are purchasing flavoured varieties, read the ingredient listing to see what is actually being added.  Is it real fruit or fruit preserves?  Read the Nutrition Facts Table to see how much sugar you are buying. 
There are many varieties with artificial sweeteners.  The calorie count on these varieties will be lower.
Another trick to reduce the sugar is to mix it up - mix plain yogurt and fruit flavoured yogurt together!

What about all the different claims that you will find on yogurt packages?

Probiotics or live or active cultures are the bacteria that help to ferment yogurt and is good for your gastrointestinal health.  Certain yogurts have specific bacteria cultures added to help regulate your digestive system.

Omega 3 -  Not a fish eater?  A yogurt with added Omega 3 may help you get some Omega 3 fatty acids.  Look at the ingredient list to ensure that it is the DHA type of Omega 3 fatty acids that is being added to the yogurt.  DHA provides many positive attributes for all stages of life.

 Fibre is added in the form of inulin which is from the chicory root.  Yogurts with fibre usually provide 2-3 grams of fibre.

 Antioxidant claims usually mean that some real fruit is present in the yogurt that will have some antioxidant properties.  Again, in a small amount of yogurt there is not going to be a lot of fruit so you will probably not receive great benefits from this claim, though having real fruit in your yogurt is great.

 If you have questions about other claims or products, please send them to me so I can help guide you to the healthy choice. Please contact me at cdombrow@hsf.ca.

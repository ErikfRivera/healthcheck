TRANS FAT AND HEART DISEASE AND STROKE

What you eat can influence you risk for developing heart disease and stroke. Trans fat from partially hydrogenated vegetable oils for example, should be limited. Trans fats makes HDL (good) cholesterol levels go down and makes LDL (bad) cholesterol levels go up. This can make the arteries more rigid; cause major clogging of arteries; and contribute to heart disease and stroke. Food manufacturers are being encouraged to limit the amount of trans fats in foods as much as possible and replace them with healthy fats. While you should try to reduce trans fat in your diet, you should also be aware of the amount and type of fats in foods and the overall number of calories in your diet. By eating foods high in fibre, following a lower-fat diet, eating lots of vegetables and fruit, and balancing what you eat with a good level of physical activity, you can reduce your risk for heart disease and stroke. FIRST WINNER OF THE CANADIAN GRAND PRIX HEALTHY
INNOVATION NEW PRODUCT AWARD ANNOUNCED

Banff, Alberta, May 30, 2004 - The Heart and Stroke Foundation of Canada's Health Check™ program and the Canadian Grand Prix New Product Awards (CGPNPA) have created a new award to highlight the food industry's commitment to bring more healthy choices to the Canadian market.

Sun-Rype Products Ltd.'s Fruit and Veggie 100% Juice is the first recipient of the Canadian Grand Prix Healthy Innovation New Product Award.   Sun-Rype's entry demonstrates exceptional value and quality to Canadian consumers and meets the Health Check program's nutrient criteria for the Vegetables and Fruit category which is based on Canada's Food Guide to Healthy Eating.  In addition to the significant exposure provided by the Canadian Grand Prix Awards Program and Canadian Council of Grocery Distributors' (CCGD) members, Sun-Rype's Fruit and Veggie 100% Juice is eligible to receive a free Health Check license for one year.

"We created this award to encourage more manufacturers to develop and launch more healthy products that are aligned with Canada's Food Guide", says Terry Dean, General Manager of the Health Check program and the Award's presenter.  Twenty-three eligible entries were submitted in 2003 for the Healthy Innovation New Product Award.

"This Award is a tangible way to celebrate the contribution of innovative food companies like Sun-Rype Products Ltd." says Dean. "The food industry is demonstrating its commitment to introducing more healthy products and that's good news for consumers."

In order to be eligible for the Award, Sun-Rype Products Ltd. submitted their products to the Grand Prix program for evaluation and had to be available to Canadian consumers in 2003.  Sun-Rype's Fruit and Veggie 100% Juice is the only product currently on the market that provides 1 serving of fruits and 1 serving of vegetables in every 250ml serving.  It combines the joint goodness of fruit and the wholesomeness of vegetables in one product that tastes great, is free of preservatives and is all natural.

The Award was presented May 30th at the Canadian Grand Prix Awards Gala during the Canadian Council of Grocery Distributors' Conference in Banff, Alberta.  Considered by many to be the "Oscars" of the food industry, the CGPNPA is a prestigious award program which celebrates innovation and excellence in new food and consumer products.

Health Check is a national not-for-profit food information program created by the Heart and Stroke Foundation to help Canadians make healthier food choices for themselves and their families.  Health Check provides consumers with a quick visual reference (a logo) on food packaging. The logo guarantees the food has met specific nutrition criteria based on Canada's Food Guide to Healthy Eating.  The program is based on guidelines and nutrition recommendations for overall health, not just heart health.   Every food in the program displays the Health Check logo and an explanatory message telling consumers why the food is part of healthy eating.  In addition, a Nutrition Facts table provides more details.

 
HEART AND STROKE FOUNDATION WARNS THAT FAT IS THE NEW TOBACCO.

The Heart and Stroke Foundation released its Annual Report Card on Canadians' Health this week warning that the increasing number of overweight and obese Canadians now poses one of the greatest threats ever to public health in this country.

According to Dr. Anthony Graham, Heart and Stroke Foundation spokesperson and cardiologist, "the prevalence of this serious health risk is almost exactly what we faced with tobacco use 30 years ago - when half of Canadians smoked."
Heart and Stroke Foundation Report Card on Canadian's Health Overweight and Smoking
Rates among Canadian adults: Early 1970's 2000/01 % change
Smoking (Aged 15+) 47% 22% 53% decrease
Overweight (BMI >25; Aged 20-64) 40% 47% 18% increase
Obese (BMI >30; Aged 20-64) 10% 15% 50% increase
*Obesity (defined as a Body Mass Index or BMI > 30) can increase a person's risk of developing heart disease or stroke by 50%.

 

When asked who they believed is responsible for finding answers to the problem of obesity, 54% of Canadians said they believe individuals are responsible, while 18% said government leadership was required. Experts in the area of obesity and overweight from across Canada said they believe that a combined effort including government policy and leadership from the food industry should play a role in reversing the trend.

The report concludes with a list of resources consumers can access to help them on their way to healthier lifestyle, including a link to the Health Check program, the Foundation's food information program.

To read the complete Report Card, visit www.heartandstroke.ca

Changing the Food Supply

Health Check helps Canadians make healthy food choices in grocery stores and restaurants and it also encourages food manufacturers and restaurants to offer more healthy food options.

As a result of stronger nutrient criteria, such as reductions in sodium and fat, many food companies and restaurants are either currently working on reduing the amount of salt and/or fat in their food products and menu items or have already done so.

What is Health Check

Health Check can help you eat well!

Choosing healthy foods can sometimes be difficult. The Heart and Stroke Foundation’s Health Check program can help you choose foods in grocery stores and restaurants that can be part of a healthy diet.

There are over 1800 Health Check products in grocery stores representing 200 brands in 75 food categories. Click here for a full list of Health Check grocery products. Health Check menu items are also available in over 800 restaurant outlets across the country. Click here for a list of participating restaurant chains.

Program Participants

The Health Check logo on a grocery product or restaurant menu item means it has met the nutrient criteria developed by the Heart and Stroke Foundation’s registered dietitians based on recommendations in Canada’s Food Guide.

Companies must earn the right to use the Health Check symbol.

Health Check is a voluntary program, open to any company or restaurant chain.

Technical Advisors

Meet our Team of Technical Advisors

Every product that displays the Health Check symbol must first meet the program's nutrient criteria. It is Health Check's Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) that develops the criteria. The committee is made up of registered dietitians and nutrition experts from across Canada who volunteer their time to Health Check.

  • Read more

Strategic Advisory Council

A new expert group was formed to provide strategic advice to the Health Check program and to help ensure its overall integrity. The Health Check Strategic Advisory Council (SAC) is comprised of leaders from across the country with a breadth of knowledge in nutrition policy, nutrition science, medicine, marketing/public relations and consumer relations.

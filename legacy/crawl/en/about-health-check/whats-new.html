March is nutrition month

An overall healthy diet is something we should strive for all year long, but nutrition month in March is a good time to stop and reflect on the choices we make, congratulating ourselves for our healthy habits and taking steps to make improvements.

The market may be fluctuating, but your appetite isn’t. Recession or not, you still need to keep yourself well nourished with healthy foods. The good news is that healthy eating doesn’t need to be expensive. Eating economically can be healthy because you’ll be relying more on fresh food and less on expensive packaged and processed foods. In general, fresh foods cost less when they are in season. Nutrition Quiz Answers

1. The four food groups are Vegetables and Fruit, Grain Products, Milk and Alternatives, and Meat and Alternatives

2. Alternatives to fluid milk include powdered or reconstituted milk, canned (evaporated) milk, fortified soy beverage, yogurt, kefir or cheese.

3. Yes, you need a certain amount of fat in your diet for healthy functioning. Oils and fats supply calories and essential fats and help our bodies absorb fat-soluble vitamins such as A, D, E and K. The type of fat consumed is as important for health as the total amount consumed.

4. The best sources of omega-3 fat are cold-water fish such as mackerel, sardines, herring, rainbow trout and salmon, as well as canola and soybean oils, omega-3 eggs, flaxseed, walnuts, pecans and pine nuts.

5. A healthy adult needs 21 to 38 grams a day, but surveys show that the average daily Canadian intake is about 14 grams. Look for foods with at least 2 grams or more of fibre per serving.

6. One serving of grain products is 1 slice of bread, half a bagel, half a pita or tortilla, half a cup of cooked rice or quinoa, 30 grams of cold cereal or 175 mL  (three quarters of a cup) of hot cereal, or half a cup of cooked pasta or couscous.

7. The Heart and Stroke Foundation suggests that Canadians eat no more than 1 tsp (5 mL) of salt each day. Remember that only about 10% of the salt we consume each day is from the salt shaker. Most of the salt we eat is hidden in processed foods, including fast foods, prepared meals, processed meats such as hot dogs and lunchmeats, canned soups, bottled dressings, packaged sauces, condiments such as ketchup and pickles, and salty snacks like potato chips.

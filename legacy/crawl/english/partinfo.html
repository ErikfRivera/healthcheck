Participation Information

Participating in the Health Check™ Program requires a five step process for every product submitted. It includes:

  • Application and product submission by manufacturer. (Certificate of analysis and review fee required.)
  • Product review by the Health Check™ Program technical managers.
  • Confirmation of product eligibility.
  • Product packaging verification by the Health Check™ Program administrators for compliance with Health Check™ Graphics Standards and federal regulations and guidelines.
  • Execution of the Licensing Agreement and Certified Product Agreement. A Licensing Fee is required at this time.

Refer to the Certification Procedure for instructions on how to proceed.

LICENCEING FEE

Cost-recovery Fee Structure
This brief section explains the Evaluation and Licencing Fees that food companies pay to off-set program costs.

In order for the Foundation to maintain its involvement, the program must become financially independent and not require any further funding from individual donations from the general public (i.e. not generate revenue for the Foundation, but eventually be self-supporting). The program costs are to be recovered through a fee paid by participating companies.

The Health Check™ program is based on a set of 25 core principles, two of which deal specifically with financing:

Cost Recovery: The program will be run on a non-profit, cost-recovery basis. The costs relating to product review, surveillance and testing, program administration, promotion, education, monitoring and evaluation will be recovered through levies on participating companies. Any surplus will be designated for nutrition education and research programs.
Consumer Pricing: The program should not result in an increase in product prices. While this is impossible to control in a free market, we strongly advise participants that the program is not to be used to leverage food prices.

Health Check™ welcomes companies of all sizes, in a variety of food categories, at levels that suit their particular needs, interests and priorities. To ensure that the program is widely accessible, the fees must be as low as possible and have been developed to be a manageable part of a company’s business plans. The expenses involved in the production, promotion and sale of food are substantial. Controlling associated costs enables products to be competitively priced and provides good value for money. To be confident that the program not be used to leverage food prices, the fee structure has been prepared with the intent of program fees being very low in relation to the cost of marketing a product. With the companies we have investigated, the License Fee is less than one percent (less than 1%) of their marketing budget for the product.

The fee structure considers many variables such as number of product formulations (recipes) and formats, number of foods in the program, extent of program involvement and market size of the company. The structure is complex and requires careful analysis to fully understand it. To make it easier to understand, a simplified version is presented below:

Fee Schedule There are two parts to the fee paid by companies: a one-time only Evaluation Fee and an annual Licensing Fee.

Evaluation Fee ($525.) - The evaluation fee is paid by any company that wishes to include a product in the program. The evaluation fee is applied to product analysis and certification.

This fee is paid when the product is first submitted for consideration. This is a one-time only fee for each product formula. Unless the company changes the product formula and requires a further analysis, it is never paid again.

Licensing Fee (ranging from $250. to $3000.) - The Licensing Fee is paid annually per product format that displays the Health Check symbol or program message. The fee helps to defray the overall program costs. In return, the company can participate in the educational and promotional aspects of the program and may display the Health Check symbol and program message on their product.

The fee structure is based on a sliding scale. This scale varies based on the size of the market in which the licenced product is sold. The scale enables product involvement for a little as $250. and no more than $3000. A product licence for product sales in a limited region would be $250. and product licence for product sales throughout Canada would be $3000.

Licensing Fee Maximums - To ensure that all companies, regardless of size, have a reasonable assessment for their involvement in the program and to limit their total potential cost, there are fee maximums. There are two types of Licensing Fee maximums:

'Food Category Maximum’ - reached when a company has fifteen (15) or more product formats in a given food category (ranging from $3,750 to $45,000).
‘Corporate Maximum’ - reached when a company has four (4) or more Food Category Maximums (ranging from $15,000 to $180,000).

Alternative (Participation) Involvement - Some companies have asked how they can participate in Health Check at the lowest possible cost. While they have expressed a desire to be involved in the program, they do not wish to share in the program’s educational and promotional aspects. For this reason, there is an option referred to as ‘Participation Rights’. With this option, the company displays a standard message on their product but does not display the Health Check symbol on product packaging, in promotional material or in product/company advertising.

For Participation Rights, companies are required to remit the Evaluation Fee (see above) and pay an annual ‘participation administration’ fee of $150.

HEART AND STROKE FOUNDATION WARNS THAT FAT IS THE NEW TOBACCO.

The Heart and Stroke Foundation released its Annual Report Card on Canadians' Health this week warning that the increasing number of overweight and obese Canadians now poses one of the greatest threats ever to public health in this country.

According to Dr. Anthony Graham, Heart and Stroke Foundation spokesperson and cardiologist, "the prevalence of this serious health risk is almost exactly what we faced with tobacco use 30 years ago - when half of Canadians smoked."
Heart and Stroke Foundation Report Card on Canadian's Health Overweight and Smoking
Rates among Canadian adults: Early 1970's 2000/01 % change
Smoking (Aged 15+) 47% 22% 53% decrease
Overweight (BMI >25; Aged 20-64) 40% 47% 18% increase
Obese (BMI >30; Aged 20-64) 10% 15% 50% increase
*Obesity (defined as a Body Mass Index or BMI > 30) can increase a person's risk of developing heart disease or stroke by 50%.

 

When asked who they believed is responsible for finding answers to the problem of obesity, 54% of Canadians said they believe individuals are responsible, while 18% said government leadership was required. Experts in the area of obesity and overweight from across Canada said they believe that a combined effort including government policy and leadership from the food industry should play a role in reversing the trend.

The report concludes with a list of resources consumers can access to help them on their way to healthier lifestyle, including a link to the Health Check program, the Foundation's food information program.

To read the complete Report Card, visit www.heartandstroke.ca

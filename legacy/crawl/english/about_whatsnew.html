Busy moms and hungry kids will have more healthy choices to choose from with Sobey's new line of co-branded products called Compliments Junior Disney. The line is geared towards children aged 3-12, available exclusively at Sobeys, IGA, Foodland and Price Chopper stores.

Eating healthy at work

 

Bringing your lunch to work can save you money, and is a much healthier option than the choices you might make in the cafeteria or the café across the street. Meals made at home tend to be healthier than store-bought options. So try making your lunch the night before as you're cleaning up from dinner. You have the right tools at your fingertips and you won't have to think about it in the morning.

Making your lunch takes a little planning and the right kind of ingredients to keep you energized all day. Here are a few lunch planning ideas:  

  • Take leftovers from dinner such as hummus, shredded carrots, and baby spinach on a whole-wheat tortilla.
  • Consider making a salad with grilled chicken (cook an extra one for dinner).
  • Other ideas include whole wheat bagel with peanut butter, apple, and sunflower seeds, peanut butter and banana sandwiches, or tuna and crackers.
  • Create your own trail mix to go along with your sandwich.
  • Try to get some balance by including servings from at least 3 or 4 food groups at every meal.
  • Drink water instead of soft drinks; it's healthier and free, particularly if your office has a water cooler.
  • If you plan to bring frozen entrees to work (example of 250 g serving size) make sure you choose entrees with:
    - Total fat: 10 g or less
    - Protein: 10 g or more
    - Sodium: 960 mg or less
  • Make a salad that can last a few lunches. For example, a can each of chickpeas, diced tomatoes, and corn, tossed with olive oil, vinegar, salt, pepper and herbs, makes enough salad for about three lunches. You can ladle it over mixed greens or baby spinach, mix in cold pasta for a pasta salad, or eat it in a pita.

 

Healthy Tips for Thanksgiving

 

Thanksgiving is a great time to enjoy some delicious meals among family and friends, but it's easy to overdo it with large portions and too many helpings. Here are some tips before you leave the house or invite the guests in:

  • Don't skip meals before Thanksgiving dinner. This will only make you hungrier and more likely to overeat at dinner.
  • Do not treat the dinner like a buffet. Fill your plate like you would a regular dinner.
  • Watch your portion sizes – one serving of meat = size of your palm.
  • Try limiting high fat items.
  • Remove the skin from your portion of turkey meat.
  • Add more fruit and vegetables on your plate and less potatoes and gravy.
  • If you're doing the cooking, use whole grain bread in the stuffing instead of white bread.
  • Invite guests to go for a walk after the meal.
